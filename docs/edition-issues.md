# Edition corrections and review policy

Record suspected and confirmed problems in the final transcription or translation as part of observation validation. Proposed corrections remain separate from the published readings.

For each issue, preserve the edition fingerprint, printed volume/page and precise locus, the current reading, the printed-source reading or interpretive evidence, the proposed correction, its effect on extracted data, and review confidence. A public correction entry should use bibliographic citations and the minimum material needed to explain the correction.

Keep these causes distinct:

- A final-edition transcription error, such as a numeral or fraction copied incorrectly.
- A translation or interpretive error affecting the meaning of a quantity, instrument, object, date, or clock.
- A publication/layout problem that obscures column relationships or omits content.
- An error or variant already present in the printed historical source.
- A parser, provenance-map, matching, or normalization defect in Tychonides.
- An error in Pafko's quotation or numerical conversion.

Use **suspected**, **confirmed**, or **rejected** issue states, with evidence for transitions. A discrepancy alone is a suspected problem, not proof that the translation is wrong. Agreement with Pafko or a modern ephemeris cannot decide which source witness is correct. Unclear print remains unresolved pending stronger evidence.

Before publishing a correction, verify it against the printed source and assess surrounding labels, the layout, and any relevant editorial qualification. Do not silently replace a historical author's number with a modern prediction. Record any adopted correction as a derivation linked to the issue and preserve the original reading.

Unclear numerals and fractional denominators require a sufficiently sharp printed witness. A discrepancy remains unresolved when the available image cannot establish the reading.
Use the best available native scan to resolve such ambiguities; enlarging a
lower-resolution derivative does not recover missing detail. Record the scan
identifier and native dimensions separately from the displayed crop size.
Map each scan to printed page numbers independently. A fixed offset requires
checked page-number anchors, with unnumbered leaves or other exceptions retained.

The [edition findings](edition-findings.md) distinguish confirmed internal discrepancies from an adopted correction; a historical author's intended number can remain unresolved even when two edition renderings demonstrably disagree.
