For a compact list of historical and modern collections and their availability,
see the [data sources directory](data-sources.md).

The [seven supplementary editions](historical-books-ingestion.md) have been ingested
alongside the four-volume Mars selection. The published books contain angular readings, recorded times, observing comments
and historical calculations used in Tychonides. They are available for direct reading,
independent transcription and computational extraction.

## Documentary lineage and review

A scan of Dreyer is evidence of the printed edition. A transcription or translation
derived from that print is another representation, not an independent historical
witness. Pafko’s independent numerical entry can expose transcription or conversion
disagreements but does not supply another observing event. A manuscript or a
genuinely different edition may adjudicate a defect already present in the print;
identify its lineage and scope rather than treating all copies as equivalent.

The published Ars Astronomica editions provide accessible source text and PDF
citations. Extraction screens and parses that text; declarative review records bind
selected expressions, contextual roles, units and relationships to evidence.
Normalization computes exact values under those interpretations. A recorded review
decision describes what was examined: text, structure, comparison entry or a
targeted scan. Neither automated parsing nor the word “reviewed” implies an
independent human image audit of every numeral. See [terminology](catalog-terms.md)
and the [processing workflow](data-workflow.md).

## Pafko’s Mars data

Wayne Pafko’s [Visualizing Tycho Brahe’s Mars Observations](https://www.pafko.com/tycho/)
includes a [manually entered spreadsheet](https://www.pafko.com/tycho/mars.xls).
It supplies an independent comparison set. Its selection does not define the complete
Mars record: the books also contain other readings, calibrations and reductions.

## Progymnasmata

The [Ars Astronomica edition](https://arsastronomica.com/works/tycho-brahe-astronomiae-instauratae-progymnasmata/)
contains related refraction tables, reference-star coordinates, solar and lunar theory,
calibration observations, and discussions of Mars’s distance and parallax. The
[original edition](https://doi.org/10.3931/e-rara-315) is held by ETH-Bibliothek Zürich.
The [historical evidence guide](historical-findings.md#astronomiae-instauratae-progymnasmata) identifies material relevant
to each research strand.

## Longomontanus: Astronomia Danica

Longomontanus’s [Astronomia Danica](https://arsastronomica.com/works/longomontanus-astronomia-danica/)
provides a numerical account of historical planetary theories, including Ptolemaic,
Copernican and Tychonic constructions and a detailed Mars theory. The
[published PDF](https://books.arsastronomica.com/Longomontanus_Astronomia_Danica.pdf)
uses the revised 1640 edition, rather than the 1622 first edition. The
[original scan](https://archive.org/details/astronomiadanica00long) supports checks
of parameters, tables and diagrams. Its observations, constructed opposition
positions and model calculations remain distinct forms of evidence.

Snellius and Riccioli reproduce material by other astronomers. Attribution belongs
to the reported observer as well as the collecting publication; a second printed
witness does not make the same observation independent evidence. Origanus's
*Introductio* supplies methods and worked examples; coverage of the daily
ephemeris volumes must be established separately before importing a time series.

## Reading the catalog alongside the books

Preserve the distinction between instrument readings, subsequent reductions, model
predictions and editorial additions. A number printed in a table need not be a new
observation. Sexagesimal fractions, clock conventions, hemispheres and source
qualifications form part of the evidence.

The [data dictionary](catalog-data-dictionary.md) explains the numerical fields;
[observation methods](observation-methods.md) explains their interpretation. The
machine-readable [four-volume bibliography](../src/data/public_sources.json)
supplies the edition, PDF and scan links used for the current catalog's volume
citations. Supplementary works retain separate source identities.

## Edition dates

The Dreyer volumes X–XIII were published in 1923, 1924, 1925 and 1926 respectively ([BnF catalog](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb318645594)). The title pages of the linked Ars Astronomica English editions state 2026. Source records retain both editions separately: `date` is the original edition year, explicitly qualified by `date_kind: original_publication_year` even when `edition_id` identifies the English translation, and `data_json.original_edition` and `data_json.translation_edition` identify the publishers, years and evidence. These are bibliographic dates, distinct from the dates of the recorded observations.
